02 / 10
Problem

Small and pop-up merchants inherit a payment stack built for banks, not counters.

→

2–3% off every sale, on top of a monthly gateway fee

Card processing margins are built for high-volume retail — they're brutal on a $5 ticket at a market stall or a one-night pop-up.

→

Money you can't touch for 2–5 business days

Settlement lag means a merchant's own cash is stuck in the rails while suppliers and rent don't wait.

→

A bank account and a POS contract just to open

Card networks require underwriting most micro-merchants and crypto-native pop-ups can't clear on day one.

→

Chargebacks that reverse a sale weeks after the fact

A completed, in-person transaction can still be clawed back — the merchant carries that risk alone.

03 / 10
Solution

The customer's wallet pays the merchant's contract. That's the whole payment stack.

No backend, no database, no custodian

The Soroban contract is the source of truth for revenue and order count — there's no server that could go down, get hacked, or hold funds in between.

Settles in the time of one Stellar ledger — about 5 seconds

The customer signs in Freighter, the contract moves the token, the merchant's balance updates before they've handed over the receipt.

Fees measured in fractions of a cent

Stellar's base fee, not a 2–3% cut — the margin a $5 ticket needs to actually be worth selling.

Opens with a wallet, not an underwriting process

Any merchant who can deploy a contract and any customer who can install Freighter can transact — no bank approval in the loop.

Market

Soroban made Stellar's payment rail programmable — commerce is the obvious next building block.

Stellar was already a payments-first chain (sub-cent fees, ~5s finality, built for remittances at scale). Soroban added smart contracts in 2024 — the missing piece was a merchant-side primitive that uses that rail directly instead of wrapping it in the old card-processing stack.
